M20 quadruped | one-page review card | simulation evidence only

  1. Where do map heights come from? From native MuJoCo multi-ray intersections with actual environment geometry.
  2. What is 2.5D mapping? Each x-y cell stores measured elevation statistics rather than a full volume.
  3. Is this SLAM? No.
  4. What is the real map update rate? Rays are integrated at 50 Hz in 16 interleaved groups.
  5. How do cells aggregate samples? The mapper stores count, sum, squared sum, mean and peak.
  6. How is cost calculated? c=0.4 clip(slope/30deg)+0.3 clip(roughness/0.04m)+0.3 clip(step/0.14m).
  7. How is the robot removed from its own scan? Geometry groups exclude robot collisions/visual meshes while retaining environmental obstacles.
  8. How does A* choose a route? Eight-neighbor edges cost distance times a risk weight.
  9. Why inflate obstacles by 0.70 m? The planned center must leave room for the robot footprint and extra tracking/slip margin.
  10. What happens if no route exists? Try reachable measured frontiers; otherwise command zero rather than guessing forward through unknown terrain.
  11. How is the curb located? Broad connected measured cells above 3.5 cm and below 14 cm indicate a low terrace.
  12. Do RGB/depth cameras control the robot? Their rendered panels are for observation.

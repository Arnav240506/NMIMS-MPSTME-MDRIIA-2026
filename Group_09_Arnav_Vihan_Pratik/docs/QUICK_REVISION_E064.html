M20 quadruped | one-page review card | simulation evidence only

  1. Why is this an M20 dog rather than a car? It has four multi-joint legs from the supplied M20 chain, 12 leg motors and four wheeled feet.
  2. How many degrees of freedom are present? There are 16 actuated joints plus a free floating base: nq=23 because the base quaternion uses four numbers; nv=22 because base rotation has three velocities..
  3. What is the actual mass? Summing the supplied XML inertias gives 34.525856 kg.
  4. How does inverse kinematics work? The two 0.25 m links use cos(knee)=(x²+z_leg²-l1²-l2²)/(2*l1*l2).
  5. What torques are applied? PD joint error, bias compensation and stance-Jacobian terms are combined, then leg torques are clipped to ±76.4 N m and hubs to ±21.6 N m.
  6. Is the balance controller MPC? No.
  7. Why climb with front and rear pairs? This follows the README strategy: lower and shift the CoM over the support pair, lift the other pair with floating wheels, plant it, move the torso and then lift the rear pair.
  8. How do you prove an actual climb? Measure from settled standing, require actual front/rear liftoff and re-support, then all four feet beyond the true lip, at plateau height, with support contacts.
  9. What was the contact bug? A rear upper-leg collision touched the step during rear transfer.
  10. How is slip measured? The logged proxy compares hub speed times wheel radius against body longitudinal speed, normalized by at least 0.10 m/s.
  11. How does skid steering turn? uL=v-w*b/2, uR=v+w*b/2, with effective track 0.55 m.
  12. Can the dog walk indefinitely? The supplied evidence verifies a 12-second diagonal-pair demonstration.
